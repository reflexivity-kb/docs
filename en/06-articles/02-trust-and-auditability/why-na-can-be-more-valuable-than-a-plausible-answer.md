# Why “NA” Can Be More Valuable Than a Plausible Answer

A complete-looking answer is not always a better answer. In investment research, an unsupported number or conclusion can create more work than an explicit statement that the required evidence is unavailable.

Showing NA at the right moment makes the evidence boundary visible and turns uncertainty into a clear next research step rather than hiding it inside fluent prose.

## Knowing where the evidence stops is useful information

General-purpose AI systems are good at producing coherent language. That is useful for many tasks, but it can create a problem when the underlying data is incomplete.

A response may read naturally even when one of the inputs needed to support the conclusion is missing. For an investor, that creates a verification burden: the answer has to be checked before it can safely inform the next step.

## An explicit gap is actionable

When a system returns an explicit unavailable result instead of filling the gap, the user knows exactly what remains unresolved.

That can lead to a better research decision: find another source, narrow the question, wait for the missing data, or treat the result as incomplete.

The absence of evidence becomes visible rather than being concealed by a polished answer.

## “NA” is part of auditability

Reflexivity's approach is to make limitations explicit when the evidence required to support a conclusion is not available.

That principle is closely related to source traceability. An answer is auditable only if the user can see both what evidence exists and where the evidence stops.

## Better research does not mean answering everything

An institutional research platform should not be judged by whether it always produces an answer.

A stronger standard is whether it produces a supported answer when the evidence is sufficient and clearly identifies the boundary when it is not.

In that context, “NA” is not a failure to answer. It is information about the state of the evidence, and that information can be more valuable than a confident-looking result that cannot be defended.

## Missing-data discipline changes downstream behavior

An unsupported answer creates hidden work. Someone must discover that a premise was missing, determine which part of the output is affected, and often rerun the analysis.

An explicit “NA” turns that hidden uncertainty into a visible research task.

That matters particularly in quantitative work. If a historical series, company field, or source document is unavailable, the correct next step may be to change the period, choose another source, or stop the calculation altogether. Filling the gap merely to complete the answer can contaminate every step that follows.

The discipline is simple: completeness is not the same as quality. In a decision-grade workflow, a clearly bounded answer is often more useful than a seamless one.
