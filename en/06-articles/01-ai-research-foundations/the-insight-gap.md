Investors have more data and AI than they can act on. The difficult part is no longer simply getting access to information.

The research bottleneck sits between information and action.

## Investors still need to identify what matters

A large research universe creates an attention problem.

Investors need to distinguish between developments that are merely interesting and those that may change an investment thesis, affect a portfolio, or deserve deeper work.

This is why prioritization matters as much as retrieval.

## Investors still need to understand how developments connect

Markets do not move through isolated facts.

A company event can affect suppliers, competitors, customers, themes, and markets. A macro development can change exposures across industries and geographies. A useful research system therefore needs to trace relationships and second-order effects, not only summarize the first event.

## Investors still need to trust the analysis enough to act

A plausible answer is not sufficient for institutional investment research.

The user needs to be able to inspect the underlying evidence, understand the limits of the available data, and decide whether the conclusion deserves confidence.

That makes traceability and explicit uncertainty part of the research workflow rather than a separate compliance step.

## The problem is synthesis, context, and trust

More data and better models are useful, but they do not remove the need to decide what deserves attention, understand why it matters, and verify the evidence.

That is the insight gap Reflexivity is designed to address.
