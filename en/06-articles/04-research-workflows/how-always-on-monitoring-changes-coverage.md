# How Always-On Monitoring Changes Coverage

Traditional coverage is constrained by human attention. Even a well-prioritized analyst cannot continuously review every company, theme, document, and event in a broad coverage universe.

Always-on monitoring changes the problem from “how often can I check?” to “which changes are relevant enough to surface now?”

## The objective is better prioritization, not more alerts

Instead of checking a company only when someone remembers to look, the system can continue monitoring relevant developments across the coverage universe.

Alfred is designed to surface portfolio-relevant events without waiting for a new prompt.

## Relevance becomes part of monitoring

More alerts are not automatically better.

The useful task is to connect detected events to the user's actual coverage and determine whether the development has a plausible investment implication.

Relationship intelligence helps with that filtering by linking events to companies, products, themes, geographies, and related exposures.

## Monitoring creates a repeatable process

A watchlist or scheduled workflow can institutionalize coverage that might otherwise depend on one analyst's attention.

That makes the research process less fragile when priorities shift or the team is busy with other work.

## Human judgment remains the decision point

Always-on monitoring is not intended to turn every detected event into an investment decision.

It is a way to make potentially relevant developments visible sooner so the investor can decide which ones deserve further analysis.

The result is broader, more repeatable coverage without requiring a human to watch every source at every moment.

## The objective is not more alerts

A monitoring system that maximizes notification volume simply transfers the filtering problem back to the analyst.

The useful objective is relevance.

That means combining event detection with context about the user's portfolio, interests, and coverage universe, then using relationship intelligence to determine whether an event has a plausible path to an exposure the user cares about.

Always-on monitoring therefore has two jobs: watch broadly and interrupt selectively.
