# From Signal Detection to Decision Support

A signal is not yet a decision. After a market event is detected, the research still has to explain what changed, identify the driver, map the business or thematic impact, and examine the related exposures.

Decision support becomes more useful when those intermediate steps remain visible, because the user can inspect the path before making the final judgment.

## Decision support is the chain between a signal and a judgment

Alfred continuously monitors market, news, document, event, and integrated data inputs to identify developments that may matter.

Detection answers the first question: what changed?

## Add relationship context

The next step is to determine what the event connects to.

Reflexivity's Knowledge Graph maps relationships across companies, products, themes, geographies, and other market entities. That context helps identify direct and second-order exposures that may not be obvious from the event itself.

## Assess the impact

Once the relevant relationships are identified, the workflow can examine business and thematic implications.

The purpose is to move beyond the headline and ask how the development may affect the companies or exposures connected to it.

## Trace the evidence

Decision support requires transparency.

Supporting evidence should be traceable to source, and missing information should remain visible rather than being filled with unsupported assumptions.

## Leave the decision with the investor

The final output should help the investor understand the event, the relevant relationships, and the evidence.

That is decision support, not automated decision-making. The system organizes and analyzes the signal so the investor can decide what deserves action.

## A four-stage chain from event to implication

The workflow can be understood in four stages:

1. detect a market event;
2. reason across the graph and source inputs to identify what is driving it;
3. assess the business and thematic impact;
4. identify the peers, suppliers, and other exposures that may be affected.

The sequence matters because it keeps the intermediate reasoning visible instead of jumping from a headline directly to a conclusion.

Each stage creates something the user can inspect: the event, the driver, the relevant relationships, and the evidence behind the possible read-through.

That is a more useful model of decision support than treating the generated conclusion as the decision itself.
