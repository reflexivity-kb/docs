# Finding the Questions You Didn’t Know to Ask

Research tools are naturally good at answering questions that have already been asked. A harder problem is noticing a development that should become a question in the first place.

Continuous monitoring is valuable when it does more than generate alerts: it connects a new event to a user's coverage and explains why the relationship may deserve investigation.

## The blind spot is often the question that has not been asked yet

A search or chat workflow begins with user intent.

That makes it effective for known questions, but silent on events that fall outside the investor's current focus. If no one thinks to ask, the research never starts.

## Monitoring can surface the trigger

Alfred continuously monitors the market and a user's coverage universe for relevant developments.

When an event is detected, relationship intelligence can help connect it to companies, products, themes, geographies, and other exposures that may matter.

The result is a potential research question that originates from the market rather than from the user's prompt.

## Second-order effects matter

The most useful question may not be “What happened to Company A?”

It may be “Which other companies are exposed to the same product, supplier, geography, or theme?” or “What does this development imply for a related part of the portfolio?”

Relationship context helps extend the investigation beyond the first-order event.

## Discovery should lead into analysis

Surfacing an unexpected signal is only the beginning.

The investor still needs to decide whether the development matters and, if it does, investigate it further. That is why proactive monitoring and on-demand analysis belong in the same workflow.

A research system becomes more useful when it can help investors not only answer more questions, but discover better questions to investigate.

## “Unknown unknowns” are a relevance problem

Monitoring everything is easy to describe and difficult to consume.

The hard part is deciding which development is relevant enough to interrupt the user.

Relationship context helps because relevance can be defined through actual exposure: a supplier relationship, a product dependency, a thematic connection, a geographic link, or another evidence-backed path.

This is also why proactive research should not be described as automatic idea generation in every case. The more defensible role is to surface developments and relationships that may deserve investigation, then let the user decide whether they change the research view.
